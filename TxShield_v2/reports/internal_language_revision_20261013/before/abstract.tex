\begin{abstract}
Decentralized Finance (DeFi) protocols have become prime targets for sophisticated attacks, with losses exceeding \$3.4 billion in 2025. However, existing detection approaches face fundamental limitations. Traditional rule-based detection tools are limited in their ability to capture diverse attack behaviors at runtime, while advanced large language models (LLMs) exhibit extrinsic limitations due to knowledge staleness and hallucinations. To this end, we observe that DeFi attacks, despite diverse implementations, exhibit recurring behavioral patterns at the semantic level, suggesting that their apparent diversity masks a small number of underlying semantic structures. In this paper, we present \approach, a Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework for real-time DeFi attack detection. \approach constructs an incrementally expandable knowledge base of historical attacks with LLM-generated behavioral summaries, employs a two-stage retrieval mechanism with dynamic similarity thresholding \Blue{to select relevant historical context}, and applies adaptive prompting to guide LLM reasoning by analogy with retrieved patterns. \Blue{An XGBoost post-filter checks the attack candidates produced by the LLM before the system returns its final result.}

\Blue{On an evaluation set constructed from 364 DeFiHackLabs attack transactions and 364 benign transactions, \approach{} achieves 92.93\% F1 and 97.87\% precision. Compared with direct XGBoost classification, it improves F1 by 6.45 percentage points and reduces false positives from 60 to 7. With the same backbone, F1 increases from 77.94\% without XGBoost to 92.93\% for the complete system. The retrieval ablation identifies a more specific trade-off: without XGBoost, two-stage retrieval increases precision but reduces recall and F1.}
\end{abstract}
